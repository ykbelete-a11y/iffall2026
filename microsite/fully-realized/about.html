About

Do Ho Suh

Every place he has lived has become a piece of his work, starting with the house he grew up in.

Early life in Seoul

Do Ho Suh was born in Seoul in 1962. His father, Suh Se-ok, was one of the best-known Korean ink painters of his generation. His mother, Min-Za Chung, helped found a nonprofit that works to preserve traditional Korean culture.

His father built the family a traditional Korean house, a hanok, partly modeled on a building at Changdeokgung Palace. It is the first house Suh recreated in fabric, and it became his picture of what “home” meant.

Education & moving

  1. 1985–1987

    Seoul National University

    Earns a BFA and MFA in Oriental painting, the ink tradition his father practiced.

  2. Late 1980s

    Military service

    Serves his required time in the Korean military, an experience of uniforms that returns in his work.

  3. 1991

    Rhode Island School of Design

    Moves to the US. Pattern-making classes teach him to sew, and he turns toward sculpture.

  4. 1997

    MFA in Sculpture, Yale University

    Completes his graduate degree. Fabric becomes his main material.

  5. 1997–2010

    New York

    Lives at 348 West 22nd Street, a home he would later recreate room by room.

  6. 2010

    London

    Settles in London, adding another home, and another set of doors and hallways, to his work.
